    3. Requirements & Rules for Logging

    Review before every practice session.

    • Driving time is capped at 2 hours per day. Any time beyond 2 hours in a day is not recorded.
    • 10 hours of the required total must be completed at night.
    • The supervisor must be 21 years of age or older, hold a valid driver's license, and sit in the front passenger seat during every session.

    4. Provisional License: Must-Dos, Do's & Don'ts

    Restrictions that apply once the Provisional License is issued.

    CURFEW: NO DRIVING between MIDNIGHT and 5:00 AM. Violations can result in serious consequences.
    PASSENGERS: Maximum of ONE passenger under 21 who is NOT a family member. Plan rides accordingly.
    ZERO TOLERANCE FOR WIRELESS DEVICES. No phone use of any kind while driving, including hands-free and voice-activated features. Put the phone away before the car moves.

    Must-Dos

    • Always wear a seat belt, and make sure every passenger does too.
    • Keep the Provisional License with you whenever you drive.
    • Obey all posted speed limits and traffic signals.
    • Maintain a safe following distance and signal every turn and lane change.

    Do's and Don'ts

    • Do plan routes ahead of time and set GPS before the car is in motion.
    • Do pull over in a safe location if you need to adjust something or respond to a message.
    • Don't drive while drowsy, upset, or impaired by alcohol, drugs, or medication.
    • Don't let distractions such as music, food, or other passengers take your attention from the road.

    Parent / Supervisor Responsibilities

    • Model safe driving at all times. Teens learn most from what they see.
    • Review each pending entry against what actually happened before approving it.
    • Discuss the curfew, passenger, and device rules before the teen drives alone.

    6. Hardship License Information (For Reference Only)

    Informational only. Confirm current eligibility and forms directly with the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS).

    • Eligibility requires documented proof of unusual economic hardship or a serious family illness.
    • Mere convenience, or a parent's work schedule, does not qualify.
